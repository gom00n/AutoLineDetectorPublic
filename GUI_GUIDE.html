LineDetector — GUI Parameter Guide

Every knob in the pipeline GUI, in plain language, with pictures — and how to label data so the results get better over time.

Four main rules
  1. The defaults are already tuned per project. Running on defaults is the right starting point — you are not doing anything wrong.
  2. Change ONE thing, then re-run and look. Tick the overlay stage and click View overlay to see the lines on the scan. Changing several knobs at once tells you nothing about which one helped.
  3. Use numbers, not just your eyes. Once you have a hand-corrected reference (see the last section), the Metrics… button gives recall / precision / F1.
  4. Think in two dials:
    Recall     = "did we catch all the real borders?"  
    Precision = "is everything we drew actually a border?" Most parameters trade one for the other.

How the pipeline works

Each stage takes the output of the one before it. Parameters only make sense once you can see where they sit in the chain — a "Skeleton" knob can't fix something the "Detection" knob got wrong.

Two map types, one engine. The only stage that differs between projects is Extract: red-ink is dark-RED ink over a yellow wash (uses a smart local threshold, no knobs in the GUI), and black-ink is BLACK ink (uses the Detection knobs below). Everything after Extract is identical for both.

The two dials: recall & precision

Almost every parameter slides you along a trade-off between catching everything and staying clean. Keep this picture in mind whenever you tune.

Detection group black-ink only

These turn the colour photo into a clean black-and-white mask of just the ink. For red-ink these three fields are blank and ignored — it uses an automatic local threshold instead.

cap black-inkdefault 145 · range ~110–170

The brightness ceiling for "this pixel is ink". A pixel counts as ink if its brightest colour channel is below cap. Black ink is dark in every channel, so this catches it and ignores coloured stuff (red numbers, purple admin lines).

SymptomDo
Faint / grey strokes are being missed (low recall)raise cap
The coloured wash or paper texture is bleeding in as fake ink (low precision)lower cap

min_px black-inkdefault 30 px

The smallest blob of ink (in pixels) the Extract stage keeps. Tiny specks — dust, ink splatter, fragments of printed digits — are smaller than this and get dropped before anything else runs.

SymptomDo
Lots of dotty speckle survives into the linesraise min_px
Short but real border bits are vanishinglower min_px

buf black-inkdefault 35 m

How far (in metres) to grow the target corridor — the Motzag polygons — before clipping. A border often sits right on the polygon's edge or just outside it; the buffer makes sure it isn't cut off. Bigger buffer = catch more edge borders, but also more surrounding clutter.

SymptomDo
Borders along the corridor edge are clipped offraise buf
Too much junk from outside the area of interestlower buf

Skeleton group both

Skeletonize thins fat ink strokes down to clean 1-pixel-wide centrelines.

close_r bothblack-ink: 1 · red-ink: 3 · 0 = off

Before thinning, "close" gaps up to this radius (pixels) so a line broken by a tiny ink fade isn't split into two. Small value = only hairline gaps; too big = fuses neighbouring lines together.

SymptomDo
Single borders keep splitting at faded spotsraise close_r (try +1)
Parallel lines are merging into one bloblower close_r

prune bothblack-ink: 15 · red-ink: 40 px

Thinning a thick or rough stroke leaves little "whiskers" hanging off the centreline. prune removes whisker branches shorter than this length (pixels). See the de-hair picture lower down — it's the same idea, here in pixels on the skeleton.

SymptomDo
Fuzzy whiskers all along the linesraise prune
Short real stubs (real corners/spurs) disappearinglower prune

Bridge group both

Bridge reconnects a border that broke into pieces — but only when the two loose ends clearly belong together, so it doesn't accidentally weld neighbouring lines.

gap bothdefault 25 px (max_gap)

cos bothdefault 0.80 (≈ within 37°)

Two rules must both pass before a gap is bridged: the ends must be closer than gap pixels, and they must point at each other within the angle set by cos (0.80 ≈ 37°; higher = must be more perfectly in line). This is what lets it rejoin a dashed border without zipping two parallel roads together.

SymptomDo
Borders stay broken across visible gaps / dashesraise gap, or lower cos toward 0.7
Lines are joining where they shouldn't (across corners, parallels)lower gap, or raise cos toward 0.9

Trace group both

Trace converts the skeleton into actual polylines and does a first cleanup pass.

spur bothblack-ink: 4 · red-ink: 12 px

Same idea as prune, but during tracing: drop a short dead-end branch (one free end, the other meeting a junction) shorter than this. Removes leftover hairs the skeleton stage missed.

loop bothblack-ink: 6 · red-ink: 30 px

When two ink lines run side by side, thinning can leave a thin closed "bubble". loop removes closed loops smaller than this. Careful: a genuinely tiny parcel is also a small loop — too high a value can erase real small parcels.

SymptomDo
Lots of little bubbles where doubled lines runraise loop
Small real parcels are being erasedlower loop

simp_px bothblack-ink: 1.0 · red-ink: 1.5 px

A pixel trace looks like a staircase. This straightens it during tracing, in pixels. There is a sister knob in metres at the very end (simp_m, default 0.5). Higher = smoother but can round off real corners.

Filter group both — the main junk remover

min_comp bothblack-ink: 40 · red-ink: 20 m

The single most important knob for artifacts. Real borders connect up into big networks; map junk — the legend, the number/area table, scattered symbols, place-name text — sits in small, isolated clusters. This keeps a line only if everything connected to it adds up to at least min_comp metres. It removes most junk automatically.

SymptomDo
Legend / text / symbols survive as stray linesraise min_comp
Short, isolated real borders are being deletedlower min_comp

Note: making the corridor tighter does not help here — it cuts real borders that sit on the corridor edge faster than it cuts junk. Size-by-connectivity is the reliable lever.

simp_m bothdefault 0.5 m

The final straightening, in metres (the metric twin of simp_px). 0.5 m removes the leftover staircase without visibly moving the lines. Raise only if outputs still look jagged; lower toward 0 if corners feel rounded off.

De-hair group both · optional, off by default

hair bothdefault 4 m (max_hair)

passes bothdefault 3

An optional final polish: snip off short "barbs" — a stub with one free end and one end on a junction — up to hair metres long. Removing one barb can expose another, so it runs passes times. Real borders and long dangles are kept.

Turn this on (tick the dehair stage) if the result is clean but slightly fuzzy at junctions. Raise hair for longer barbs — but too high starts eating short real spurs.

Snap group both · optional, off by default

snap_tol bothred ink: 3 · black-ink: 8 m (tol)

snap_frac bothdefault 0.6 (match_frac — short lines only)

snap_run bothdefault 10 m (min_run)

Optional. All the reference layers (Snap layers from the corridor GDB + Parcels lyr from the parcels GDB) are pooled into one boundary network. Every stretch of a detected line that follows that network within snap_tol metres — for at least snap_run metres — is moved exactly onto it; the rest of the line keeps the drawn ink. A line can therefore be split: half snapped, half historical. Reference boundaries crossing the line at a steep angle are ignored (no zig-zag "teeth"). Lines shorter than snap_run are snapped whole only if at least snap_frac of their length is on the network.

SymptomDo
Lines that clearly match the reference aren't being cleaned upraise snap_tol, or lower snap_run
It's "correcting" lines onto the reference that shouldn't movelower snap_tol, or raise snap_run

The output _lines_snapped.shp gets a snapped field: 0 = kept as ink, 1 = on a reference boundary. The preview PNG colors them red / green. Filter on the field in Pro to review what changed.

Inputs & corridor

Mode

Picks the project preset: black-ink (black ink) or red-ink (red ink over a yellow wash). All projects use the same engine — the target area is always the GDB polygons whose Motzag equals the sheet number — the modes differ only by ink method and default folders/GDBs. Switching mode reloads that preset's defaults.

Sheets

What to process. Accepts a single name (M8_9076), a comma/space list, a wildcard (M2_*), a folder path (every sheet in it), or all. Scan fills this with every sheet in the Input folder; Pick files… lets you tick them.

Corridor GDB & GDB layers black-ink

The geodatabase holding the target polygons, and which layers inside it to use. The pipeline reads only the polygons whose Motzag attribute equals the sheet number (so M8_9076 → Motzag = 8), buffers them (buf), and only looks for ink there. Use Choose… to tick the layers. If a sheet has no matching polygons it is skipped (not an error).

Snap layers

Which layer(s) in the corridor GDB to snap onto — the block/Gush polygons (e.g. Poly_ID, Poly_Map, Poly_map2). Use Choose…. Only matters if you run Snap.

Parcels GDB & Parcels lyr

A separate GDB holding the clean cadastral parcels (e.g. Cad-GOV\Layers.gdb → PARCEL_ALL). Pooled with the Snap layers into one reference network. Reprojected automatically. Only matters if you run Snap.

Cheat sheet — "I see X, change Y"

What you see in the overlayMost likely knob

Borders broken into pieces / dashed gaps not closedgap ↑, cos ↓ · close_r ↑
Place-name text, legend, number table showing as linesmin_comp ↑

Rule of thumb: artifacts (text, symbols, fold, hatching) are almost always a min_comp / connectivity problem first. Try that before anything else.

Labeling data to make results better & fight artifacts

Tuning by eye plateaus. To push further you give the pipeline a little hand-made "truth" to learn from and measure against. Three tiers, easiest first.

Tier 1 — a reference "truth" layer (do this first; it powers Metrics)

This turns guessing into measuring.

  • Copy the auto-result <stem>_lines.shp into a new editable feature class <stem>_ref — you correct a near-complete trace, you don't draw from scratch.
  • Don't do a whole sheet. Pick 2–4 small windows (a few hundred metres each): a clean rural block, a built-up/town block, a text-heavy patch, an area over the wash/fold. In each: delete false lines, draw the missing borders snapped to the ink at full zoom, fix wandering traces.
  • Save in EPSG 6991 (same as the result — Metrics does not reproject).
  • Before scoring, clip the result to the same windows (Pairwise Clip, available on Basic) — otherwise precision looks falsely bad from all the lines outside the labeled area.

Unlocks: the Metrics… button. Low recall = missing real lines (loosen). Low precision = artifacts (the thing to drive down). Now every knob change gets a number.

Tier 2 — labeled artifact boxes (the artifact-fighting engine)

A polygon layer artifacts_<stem>: draw a box around each recurring artifact and tag it with a kind attribute. 5–10 boxes per kind is plenty.

kindwhat it isthe signal that separates it from a real border
textplace names / parcel numbersshort, very wiggly strokes packed into a small box
building_blocktown hatchingmany short right-angle segments at a regular spacing
double_lineroad/river drawn as a pairtwo near-parallel lines a fixed distance apart
foldscan creaseone very long, very straight line crossing the sheet
symbol / gridlegend, north arrow, graticulefixed place / regular geometry

Unlocks: for each kind I can measure how much a candidate filter removes of that artifact versus how much real border it costs, then design and wire in a targeted filter. Sending me the boxes (or even just a screenshot or two) is exactly what lets me give you "this is the bucket, here's the filter".

Tier 3 — keep/drop tags per line (a learned filter; optional, highest ceiling)

Tag a few hundred traced components on one sheet with keep = 1/0. That's a training set for a small classifier (length, straightness, number of junctions, ink darkness, distance to the nearest modern parcel edge…) that decides "real border or not" — generalizing all the hand-set thresholds into one learned rule. Runs on the Basic license, no Spatial Analyst. If you want this, I'll add two small scripts to export the features and train/apply the model.

(Tier 4, only if the classical approach stalls: rasterize the Tier-1 reference into a border mask, cut into tiles, train a U-Net. Heaviest path — revisit only if 1–3 aren't enough.)
